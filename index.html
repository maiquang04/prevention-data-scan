What data already exists for measuring prevention?

Prevention work is easy to argue for and hard to evidence, because the data that would show whether it worked sits in a lot of different places – and some of it cannot be obtained at all. This is a scan of what has already been published, and an honest assessment of which of it you could actually get hold of.

How to read this

Two questions get confused with each other, so they are kept apart here. Can you read the paper? is not the same as can you get the data? Plenty of good research sits on data nobody outside the original team can obtain. Every source carries a badge saying which situation it is in:

    What this is, and what it is not

    This is a scan of published work – papers, government reports and data collections – carried out at the University of Queensland. It records what other people have already measured, how they measured it, and whether the underlying data can be obtained.

    It is not an analysis of Queensland data, and it does not contain any health statistics of its own. Nothing on this site is confidential: every source listed is already published, and every summary is written here from scratch rather than copied.

    It is also incomplete on purpose. Some sources have been profiled in detail and some have only been read; where the data behind a source has not been checked yet, it says so rather than guessing.
